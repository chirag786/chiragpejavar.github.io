Completeness had to come before sophistication

The problem

Multi-account investors were assembling their net-worth picture by hand, out of spreadsheets and statement downloads. RIISE could see what a user held with Motilal Oswal and nothing else, so every analysis it offered was analysis of a fragment.

What I found

The sequence mattered more than the feature. Completeness had to come first: once a user’s holdings were whole, concentration and exposure warnings landed as useful rather than alarming, because they implied a specific next move.

A risk warning is only worth showing once the user agrees you can see everything.

What changed

Two of the concepts I originated went into the roadmap: aggregation of externally held assets, and a risk-and-concentration layer over the combined view. I restructured how risk is surfaced on screen, and stayed through launch to run the post-launch impact analysis rather than handing over at the insight.

Products that worked and were not seen, or were seen and not understood

That was the recurring pattern across both companies. Fixed income is where I met both halves of it.

Upstox: buried, not rejected

Funnel evidence showed that non-convertible debentures were not being turned down. They were not being found: interest was created, then lost before it became an application. I recommended greater visibility and promotion, and argued placement from the point in the funnel where the drop happened rather than from where the product sat on the roadmap. I partnered with the Strategy team on rollout for new NCD launches.

Alongside it, the CSAT study on the bonds buying journey surfaced comprehension and trust gaps in the flow. Journey fixes were then prioritised by satisfaction impact rather than by effort.

Motilal Oswal: the language did not survive the user

I met the same pattern at a larger scale, this time with the remit to fix it structurally. I ran the buying journey end to end with non-specialist investors, probing comprehension at each decision point: yield versus coupon, credit rating, lock-in, maturity.

The interest was there. The language was not surviving contact with a retail user. I rebuilt the section strategy around plain-language risk and return, and argued for placement matching the demand the research had shown.

Appetite was never the constraint. Comprehension was, both times.

A feed with reach and no pull

The problem

The in-app news feed was reaching users and not moving them. There were strong opinions in the room about why. Rather than argue, I audited every item against one test: could a user say why this matters to them specifically. Then I tested variants instead of defending a position.

What I found

Relevance beat both volume and recency. Two kinds of item carried the engagement, and only two. Items tied to a holding or a watchlist entry, and items that stated their consequence rather than the event.

What changed

The feed was rebuilt around those two rules. I also changed what counted as success: clicks measured curiosity, which the feed already had plenty of. Repeat sessions measured whether it had become a reason to reopen the app. We measured on repeat sessions.

Where interest died before it became an action

The problem

Interest was being created on the web platform and lost somewhere before it turned into an account action. The platform had traffic and intent. What it did not have was a path between the two.

What changed

I traced where the drop was happening, then designed and implemented a restructured engagement funnel across the platform. This is where my work went furthest past the recommendation: I owned the funnel design and its rollout, not just the diagnosis. The growth held for a full quarter rather than spiking and settling.

A note on the numbers above

How these were measured, and what they are not

Every figure on this page is a post-launch delta: the change measured after a shipped change, against the period before it. None of them come from a controlled A/B test.

That was a deliberate trade. Maintaining two parallel designs was outside the team’s bandwidth, and the decisions these studies fed were on product deadlines. I chose single-variant testing with larger samples and faster turnaround over cleaner attribution, because a clean answer that arrives after the decision is not worth much.

Where attribution is shared, I say contributed rather than caused. The AUM figure is a contribution to a launch, not an isolated causal claim about my research.

Happy to walk through the baseline, the window and the instrument for any study here, including the ones where the finding was inconvenient.
